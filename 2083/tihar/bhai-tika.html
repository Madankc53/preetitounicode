Bhai Tika 2083 date and time

Date: Wednesday, November 11, 2026, which is Kartik 25, 2083 BS. Bhai Tika always falls on Kartik Shukla Dwitiya, the second day of the bright lunar fortnight. Published Nepali calendars agree on this date.

Time (sait): The tika time is set by the official Nepali patro and is not the same in every calendar. Most families complete the ceremony between mid-morning and early afternoon. Look up the sait in the Nepal Panchanga Nirnayak Samiti calendar before the day, and follow your family priest if you have one.

Tip: if you are searching "bhai tika time", the date is fixed (Nov 11) but the exact minute depends on the patro your family follows.

What is Bhai Tika?

Bhai Tika (also spelled Bhaitika or Vai Tika) is the festival of the sister-brother bond and the last day of Tihar, Nepal's festival of lights. Sisters apply a seven-coloured tika on their brothers' foreheads, place a garland around their necks, and offer sweets, fruits and dry fruits. Brothers give gifts or money in return. Many families also include cousins and relatives, so a man without a sister can receive tika from a cousin or an aunt.

The story behind it. In the legend, Yamuna invited her brother Yama, the god of death, applied tika on his forehead and fed him. Pleased, Yama blessed her that any brother who receives tika from his sister on this day will live a long life. The festival continues that wish.

How Bhai Tika is done

  1. The family prepares the puja plate with tika colours, oil, garlands, fruits, sweets and sel roti.
  2. The brother sits facing the sister at the sait (the tika time from the patro).
  3. The sister performs the rituals, often circling the brother with oil and water, and prays for his long life.
  4. She applies the seven-coloured tika and places the garland. Brothers then do the same for sisters in many families.
  5. Gifts, money and a shared meal follow, with Deusi-Bhailo singing groups visiting in the evening.

Details of the ritual and the colours used vary between communities. Newars celebrate the day as Kija Puja, and Madhesi communities as Bhardutiya.

Is Bhai Tika a holiday in Nepal?

Yes. Bhai Tika is a public holiday in Nepal. Government offices, banks and schools are closed, and travel is heavy as families return home. See the full list on the Nepal public holidays 2083 page, and confirm with the Ministry of Home Affairs notice.

Bhai Tika vs Bhai Dooj

Bhai Tika in Nepal and Bhai Dooj in India both celebrate siblings on the second day after Diwali, and both fall on November 11, 2026 this year. The main difference is the tika. In Nepal the sister applies a long seven-coloured tika, a garland and the full Tihar offerings; in much of India the tilak is simpler.
